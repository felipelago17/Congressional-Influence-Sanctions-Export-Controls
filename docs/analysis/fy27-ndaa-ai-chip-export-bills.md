## Why this note exists

The NYT story supplies the first concrete, named evidence that the administration's 2026 H200 licensing policy approved Chinese end users that fail basic vetting (empty registered offices; ownership links to listed parties). It arrived four days after the midterms handed Democrats both chambers effective 3 January 2027, and in the window in which the FY2027 NDAA must be conferenced or die. That makes it a textbook **congressional-signal event**: a press revelation that advocates immediately converted into demands on BIS and the NDAA conferees. This note records the demands, the bills they map to, and what primary evidence would be needed before any later agency action can be graded as linked.

The LinkedIn commentary (Tier 3) sets out a four-addressee action list. Each item is mapped here to the lever taxonomy in [Administration Levers](../influence/administration-levers.md) so that any subsequent action can be matched against it.

| Addressee | Ask | Lever | Primary source that would evidence follow-through |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commerce / BIS | Close the remote-access gap now with **is-informed letters** pending a structural fix | Administrative — informal licensing requirement (EAR § 744.21-style notice) | Letters are not published; evidence would be a BIS statement, a recipient's SEC disclosure, or a later rule preamble citing the practice |
| Commerce / BIS | Add **Megaspeed** to the Entity List | Administrative — Entity List rule | Federal Register final rule; ERC record |
| Commerce / BIS | Policy of **denial for further H200 licences to China** | Administrative — licensing policy change | BIS rule amending § 742/§ 744 licensing policy, or published guidance |

| Nvidia | "Pick a side" | n/a — private-sector pressure | n/a |

None of these is an observed action as of 8 Nov 2026. They are recorded so that if BIS acts in the coming weeks, the temporal and topical proximity to this signal is already documented — which, under the [Linkage Grades](../methodology/linkage-grades.md) rules, still yields at most `proximity-only` unless an agency document cites the pressure.

## Assessment against the repository's framework

1. **Oversight, not legislation, is the realistic near-term channel.** As the [Sanctions Drop #18 note](sanctions-drop-18-democrats-take-congress.md) argued, the midterm result changes hearing and letter capacity, not filibuster arithmetic. The NYT story is well suited to oversight letters to Commerce demanding the licensee list — expect those from both parties, since the signatories to the June coalition letter span the spectrum.
2. **The NDAA is the only statutory vehicle left in the 119th.** If the three Senate-amendment bills survive conference, each becomes a `statutory-mandate` anchor for later BIS rules (location verification, congressional review of top-tier licences, SME alignment). If RASA is dropped, a BIS remote-access rule in 2027 would have to be graded against a reintroduced 120th-Congress bill.
3. **Administrative substitutes are likely regardless.** Is-informed letters and an Entity List action on Megaspeed are within BIS's existing authority and need no legislation. Note the asymmetry: the same week, BIS **extended the Affiliates Rule suspension to 10 January 2027** (final rule filed 5 Nov; FR 9 Nov), the opposite direction from what the commentary urges — a reminder that trade-truce management is currently the dominant executive constraint on China-facing controls.
4. **Partisan coding.** Sponsors are bipartisan (Lawler, Moolenaar, Banks, Cotton, Ricketts, Kim, Baumgartner, Goodlander). This does not fit a party-influence narrative; it fits the China-committee / HFAC institutional-influence pattern recorded in [Party and Committee Aggregates](../influence/party-committee-aggregates.md).
