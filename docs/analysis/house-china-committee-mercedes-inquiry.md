## What is reported

- The **House Select Committee on China's** Republican chair and ranking Democrat asked Mercedes-Benz about two dozen questions on its Chinese shareholders (about 20 % combined: BAIC nearly 10 %, and Geely's chair nearly 10 %), their influence, technology partnerships (Hesai, Tencent, Momenta), Chinese-made components and contacts with Chinese officials; they also want documents and a meeting with the North America CEO.
- Reuters calls the letter "previously unreported" and seen by it; it says the request came "on Wednesday" under a 17 Nov dateline, while VOA dates the letter 16 Nov. **Deadline: 1 Dec 2026** (both sources).
- The letter says the answers would help Congress as it considers legislation against Chinese vehicles.
- Legislation approved by **Senate Commerce in July** would bar companies more than **15 %** owned by Chinese entities from selling vehicles in the US (Reuters); VOA names it the Connected Vehicle Security Act and says it has made little progress.
- A Republican senator on the bill (Bernie Moreno) said the final text will ensure Mercedes-Benz is not banned, that talks cover keeping Volvo Cars (Geely-owned) on the market, and that he hoped for action as soon as that week.

## Assessment against the repository's framework

1. **An oversight action directed at a private company, not an agency.** No administration action is cited, so no linkage grade applies; this is a signal entry only.
2. **Bipartisan and committee-driven.** Consistent with the repository's finding that China-committee actions do not follow a party narrative (compare the [NDAA note](fy27-ndaa-ai-chip-export-bills.md)).
3. **Stated legislative purpose.** The letter ties its questions to pending vehicle legislation (per Reuters's quotation). That is a documented purpose, not a documented request from an agency, so it is not a `documented-request` linkage.
4. **Bill design is being negotiated.** The 15 % threshold would on its face reach Mercedes-Benz, yet a sponsor says the final text will not; the gap suggests exemptions or a different trigger. Nothing read shows the drafting.
5. **Timing.** Action "as soon as this week" and automaker lobbying for passage in the final weeks of the Congress make the year-end package a plausible vehicle; no source read confirms it.
6. **Executive context.** A Biden-era 2025 rule already bars Chinese automakers; the President's September remarks on Chinese plants in the US are a cross-pressure. Temporal proximity is not evidence of causation and none is inferred.
7. **Unknowns.** The letter text, Commerce's view, and the bill text.
