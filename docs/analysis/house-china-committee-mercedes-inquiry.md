## What is reported

- The **House Select Committee on China's** Republican chair and ranking Democrat sent Mercedes-Benz a letter dated **16 Nov 2026**, released **17 Nov**, asking about Chinese shareholders (reportedly about 20 % combined, including BAIC and entities tied to Geely's founder), their influence, and technology partnerships (Hesai, Tencent, Momenta), Chinese-made components in US-sold vehicles, and contacts with Chinese officials.
- **Deadline: 1 Dec 2026** (VOA; one aggregator says 6 Dec), with a document-preservation request.
- Stated purpose: oversight of national-security and supply-chain risks and input to **legislation on Chinese cars**.
- The **Connected Vehicle Security Act** (bipartisan Senate bill) would bar connected-vehicle trade with entities tied to China and other adversaries and bar automakers more than **15 % owned** by such entities; reported to have cleared Senate Commerce in July with little progress since; a similar House bill exists.

## Assessment against the repository's framework

1. **A congressional oversight action directed at a private company, not at an agency.** No administration action is cited, so no linkage grade applies. It is a `signal` entry only.
2. **Bipartisan, committee-driven.** Consistent with the repository's finding that China-committee and Foreign Affairs-type actions do not follow a party-influence narrative (compare the [NDAA note](fy27-ndaa-ai-chip-export-bills.md)).
3. **Scope.** The request concerns ownership, influence and technology sharing, the same themes as the repository's tracked rules (OFAC 50 % rule, BIS Affiliates Rule), but it is **not itself a sanctions or export-control action**; its connection is topical.
4. **Legislative pathway.** The inquiry's stated aim is to inform legislation on Chinese vehicles; the Connected Vehicle Security Act's **15 % ownership trigger** would reach automakers such as Mercedes-Benz if enacted (per VOA). Whether it moves before the 119th Congress ends on 3 Jan 2027 is **not shown**; the year-end defence bill is a possible vehicle, but no source read says so.
5. **Unknowns.** The letter text, the Commerce Department's view, and any administration response are not covered.
