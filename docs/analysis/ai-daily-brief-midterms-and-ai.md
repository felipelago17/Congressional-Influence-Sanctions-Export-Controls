## What the episode says (covered portion)

**Result as framed.** A Democratic wave with an ~11-point national swing, control of both chambers, no veto-proof supermajority; smaller in seat terms than 2010 or 2018 but with flips in Texas, South Carolina and Kentucky. The host's correction to the "AI was on the ballot" framing: *data centres* were on the ballot, not AI as such.

**Polling the host cites.**
- Pew (early October): 44% of voters called AI a very important issue — below foreign policy (57%), immigration (59%), education (62%), health care (68%), democracy (73%) and the economy (84%). The issue skewed Democratic (54% vs 34%).
- Pew on data centres specifically: 61% dislike or strongly dislike candidates who support local data-centre construction (75% of Democratic supporters, 44% of Republican); only 5% and 14% respectively *like* such candidates. His reading: supporting data centres was a vote-loser for both parties.
- NBC exit poll: 56% called data centres a crucial or major factor (Democrats twice as likely as Republicans), against immigration enforcement 73%, the Iran war 74%, cost of living 89%, and government corruption 90% — i.e. last among the hot-button issues but high enough to be polled.

**How it was campaigned.** Data-centre opposition was folded into economy and "oligarchy" themes rather than run as a tech issue: Sherrod Brown (Ohio) on Ohioans not subsidising costs and deciding locally rather than "billionaire friends"; Abdul El-Sayed (Michigan) tying Flock cameras and data centres to Mike Rogers' Patriot Act vote. Republicans were boxed in by the President's late-August Truth Social line that communities rejecting data centres would end up "backwards and poor"; few split from him, and incumbents like Jon Husted defaulted to "a local decision."

**Races where AI was central.**
- *California 11th (San Francisco)* — Scott Wiener (SB 1047 sponsor; kill-switch, developer liability, frontier-model threshold; vetoed by Newsom but seeding later state laws) leading Connie Chan (Pelosi-endorsed) at recording time. Wiener campaigned on having "gone to war with big tech"; Chan attacked his $3m from an Anthropic-aligned pro-regulation PAC. The host's take: Wiener is positioned as a champion for existential-risk-focused AI-safety advocates, not an industry ally.
- *Ohio* — ~200 existing data centres, 46 planned at a combined 16 GW, led by Meta's Prometheus site outside Columbus. (Transcript ends here.)

## Why it is filed here

- **Data-centre siting and chip controls intersect.** Where compute is built, and under what conditions, is the domestic face of the question export controls address abroad: who gets access to advanced compute. Congressional pressure on siting, energy and water may arrive in the same committees as remote-access and chip-security bills.
- **Oversight capacity is fungible.** The oversight gains all post-midterm sources agree on ([cross-source check](midterms-2026-cross-source-check.md)) can be spent on AI hearings as readily as on sanctions waivers; which gets priority is itself a signal.
- **Member-level AI positions are relevant context, not scorecards.** The repository aggregates by party and committee only; Wiener's and Brown's positions are noted here because the host made them central, and they will matter for committee assignments — not as individual ratings.
- **Keyword scan coverage.** `artificial intelligence` and `semiconductor` are already in the Congress.gov watchlist keywords; bills this commentary anticipates will surface automatically.
