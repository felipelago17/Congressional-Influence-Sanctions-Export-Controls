## What is reported

- A **Dutch Ministry of Foreign Affairs** spokesperson told Politico the main concern is the bill's extraterritorial character: each country is responsible for its own national-security and export-control policy, and such measures are "unacceptable between partners".
- Press summary of the bill: allies including the Netherlands and Japan get **150 days** to align restrictions on sales **and servicing** of advanced chipmaking equipment in China, or show progress; otherwise **Commerce must extend US controls unilaterally** to equipment from those countries. It would extend controls to more DUV tool types and tighten servicing of machines already in China.
- **Dutch diplomacy:** the Prime Minister raised concerns in Washington in April; the Foreign Trade Minister raised them with the Commerce Secretary and lawmakers in June. **ASML's CFO** criticised the bill in October. China was nearly a third of ASML's revenue last year.
- **European voices:** a Dutch MEP and the chair of the European Parliament trade committee criticised the US approach; Berlin is reported to share concerns.

4. **The executive–legislative tension is the structural point.** The article's analysis is that a statute would reduce executive discretion that Commerce currently holds. That frames MATCH as a **congressional lever against administrative discretion**; see [Administration Levers](../influence/administration-levers.md). It is the outlet's analysis, not a recorded position of any member.
5. **Contrast with the administrative track.** The BIS [remote-access rule](bis-remote-access-ifr-and-rasa.md) acts by rule; MATCH needs Congress, and allied resistance is directed at the statutory mechanism.
6. **Unknowns.** The Japanese position, the Commerce Department's view, and any named member's public response are **not covered**. Whether the Dutch statement affects NDAA conference text is **not shown** by any source read.
