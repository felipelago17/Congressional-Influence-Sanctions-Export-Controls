**Pattern.** Every case is an *intermediary* case — a server integrator, reseller or forwarder, never the chipmaker. The enforcement surface is the point where a licensed U.S. product is repackaged. That is the factual world the Remote Access Security Act and chip-location bills are written for, which is why these indictments recur in congressional letters and hearing questions; no documented linkage to a specific administration action has yet been recorded.

## Reading across the year

1. **Semiconductors and Entity List diversion are the priority** at BIS (Applied Materials, Bosch, Exyte) and DOJ (all four chip cases). China is the counterparty in every one.
2. **Extraterritorial hooks are doing the work.** Bosch (FDPR — foreign-made goods, foreign shipper), Adani (dollar clearing), Extreme (50 percent rule). None of the three largest corporate cases turned on a U.S. person exporting a U.S. item to a listed party in the plain sense.
3. **Self-disclosure timing is now the decisive variable** under the CEP, and OFAC's Part 505 makes disclosure status a public fact in every settlement.
4. **Iran enforcement reaches energy trading** (Adani LPG) — relevant to any Gulf trading counterparty screening Iranian-origin cargo under disguised provenance.
5. **Statutory-maximum penalties are being used** (Applied Materials) alongside suspended denial orders — a structural, not only monetary, deterrent.

## Case note — 2026-10-27, Extreme Networks (DOJ NSD / OFAC)

**What happened.** DOJ's National Security Division and the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of North Carolina announced a three-year non-prosecution agreement with Extreme Networks over 2018–2022 sales of networking equipment and services, through a since-closed Russian subsidiary and a distributor, to Moscow Machinery Building Plant Avangard (MMZA), a Russian air-defence manufacturer. OFAC announced a parallel $1,529,685 civil settlement the same day.

**Why it matters for this tracker.**

- **First "near-miss" NPA under the revised CEP.** DOJ found the self-disclosure not timely (it came days before an investigative journalist published), so a declination was unavailable; cooperation and remediation still produced an NPA with no monitor. Compare the June 2026 Bosch declination, where disclosure during an ongoing internal investigation counted as timely. The term is three years, longer than the CEP's "fewer than three years" guidance for near-miss cases.
- **50 percent rule in practice.** MMZA appears on no list. It is blocked only by operation of OFAC's 50 percent rule as a majority-owned subsidiary of designated JSC Concern Almaz-Antey — a concrete enforcement example for the ownership-aggregation thread tracked under [regulations under discussion](../index.md#current-baseline-november-2026).
- **No individual protection.** As in Bosch, the NPA does not shield individuals.
