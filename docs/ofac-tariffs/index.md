# OFAC and Tariffs — where sanctions and trade remedies meet

**Status as of 5 November 2026.** Not legal advice.

This section tracks the convergence of two instruments that used to sit in different agencies and different statutes: **sanctions** (OFAC, under IEEPA and programme-specific statutes) and **tariffs** (USTR/Commerce/CBP, under Title 19 trade statutes). Since 2025 the executive has tried to use the sanctions statute to impose tariffs, the Supreme Court has said it cannot, and Congress has responded by writing tariff authority directly into a sanctions act. For a repository about congressional influence over sanctions and export controls, this is the clearest live example of the legislature reclaiming a lever.

## The legal framework in one table

| Instrument | Statute | Who administers | Congressional control point | Post-*Learning Resources* status |
|------------|---------|-----------------|-----------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Blocking, licensing, trade prohibitions ("sanctions") | IEEPA, 50 U.S.C. §1701 et seq.; programme statutes (CAATSA, etc.) | OFAC (Treasury), State | Emergency declarations reviewable under NEA; programme statutes can mandate or constrain | **Untouched.** *Learning Resources* is confined to tariffs. |
| Tariffs under emergency powers | IEEPA ("regulate … importation") | President → CBP | — | **Foreclosed.** IEEPA does not authorise tariffs (6–3, 20 Feb 2026). |
| National-security tariffs | Section 232, Trade Expansion Act 1962 | Commerce investigation → President | Investigation and reporting requirements; appropriations | Available; procedural steps required |
| Unfair-practice tariffs | Section 301, Trade Act 1974 | USTR investigation → President | Investigation, findings, consultation | Available |
| Balance-of-payments tariffs | Section 122, Trade Act 1974 | President | 15% / 150-day cap unless Congress extends | Available, time-limited |
| Discrimination tariffs | Section 338, Tariff Act 1930 | President | Up to 50%; rarely used | Available, untested |
| **Secondary tariffs on buyers of Russian energy** | **Graham Act, Pub. L. 119-111, §§112–113** | President; USTR determines top-5 lists every 180 days | **Statutory mandate** with §115 waiver subject to certification and report to Congress; §117 termination conditions | **Enacted and waived** (EO of 16 Oct 2026) |
| "Secondary tariffs" on buyers of Venezuelan/Iranian oil | IEEPA (EO 14245 and successors) | State/Commerce determinations | — | **Unlawful** as tariff measures after *Learning Resources*; never applied |

## Why this matters for the repository's method

1. **It is a clean statutory-mandate case.** The Graham Act is a congressional signal that became law with large bipartisan majorities, mandates specific executive actions on a 30-day clock, and then was waived under an authority Congress itself wrote. Every step is documentable from Tier 1 sources — see [`linkages.yaml`](https://github.com/felipelago17/Congressional-Influence-Sanctions-Export-Controls/blob/main/trackers/linkages.yaml).
2. **It shows the judiciary as a third actor.** *Learning Resources* changed the baseline between the Act's introduction and its enactment; the Act's drafters explicitly routed the tariff power through Article I to survive the ruling. Recorded under [judicial bounds](../influence/administration-levers.md#judicial-bounds-on-delegated-authority).
3. **It is the hinge for post-midterm commentary.** Whether a Democratic Congress can "do something about tariffs" turns on this framework: sanctions authority is intact and executive; tariff authority is now statute-by-statute and procedurally gated, which is what makes it congressionally contestable. See the [cross-source check](../analysis/midterms-2026-cross-source-check.md).

## What to watch

- Any §115 waiver being narrowed or allowed to lapse (open-ended as drafted — no expiry, no review period).
- USTR's first top-5 determinations under §113 (due 180 days after initial imposition; currently moot while waived).
- Bills constraining §115 or nullifying OFAC General License 135 (Russian diesel) — two House proposals reported by practitioners; not yet on the keyword scan.
- Litigation testing *Learning Resources*' clear-statement reasoning against other IEEPA uses (quotas, de minimis suspension — the dissent flagged quotas as the next question).
- Refund litigation at the Court of International Trade over IEEPA duties collected before 24 February 2026.
