# Administration Levers

Observable channels through which Congress can affect administration actions on sanctions and export controls. All entries are based on public records only.

## Levers tracked

- **Statutory mandates** — enacted law that directs or constrains agency action (ECRA, CAATSA, NDAA provisions, etc.).
- **Appropriations** — funding conditions or report language.
- **Oversight** — hearings, questions for the record, committee letters.
- **Nominee holds** — public holds on Treasury or Commerce nominees.
- **Mandatory reports** — statutory reporting requirements and their deadlines.
- **GAO / CRS / OIG** — reports cited by agencies or committees.

## Judicial bounds on delegated authority

Court decisions that fix the outer limit of what the executive may do under a statute Congress passed are recorded here, because they change the baseline against which every congressional signal is read. They are Tier 1 sources in their own right.

| Decision | Holding | Why it matters here |
|----------|---------|---------------------|
| *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump*, No. 24-1287 (U.S. 20 Feb 2026), consolidated with *Trump v. V.O.S. Selections, Inc.*; 6–3, Roberts C.J. — [opinion text (Justia)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/607/24-1287/) | The International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) does not authorize the President to impose tariffs; tariffs are a tax reserved to Congress under Art. I §8, and IEEPA's power to "regulate … importation" does not reach them. The D.D.C. parallel case was dismissed for want of jurisdiction (exclusive to the Court of International Trade). | (1) Removes the emergency-powers route to tariffs; subsequent tariffs rest on Section 232/301 and similar statutes with their own procedural steps, which are easier for Congress to condition. (2) IEEPA is also the statutory base for most OFAC sanctions programmes. The holding is confined to tariffs and does not touch blocking, licensing or trade-restriction powers — but its reasoning (major-questions / clear-statement) is the kind of argument litigants will test against other IEEPA uses. (3) Explains why "Congress can retake tariff policy" is a live hypothesis in post-midterm commentary (see the [cross-source check](../analysis/midterms-2026-cross-source-check.md)) while sanctions authority is not. |

Secondary summaries: [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/briefings/2026/02/ieepa-tariffs.html), [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/brookings-experts-on-the-supreme-courts-tariff-decision/), [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/20f2de87/potential-refunds-us-supreme-court-overturns-ieepa-tariffs) (Tier 3; the IEEPA tariffs had been rescinded on 7 February 2026, before the ruling, which bears on refunds).

## Status fields used

Every related action records:

- `scheduled`
- `announced`
- `published`
- `in_force`

An announcement is never treated as a published rule.
