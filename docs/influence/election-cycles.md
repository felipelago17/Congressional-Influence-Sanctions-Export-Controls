# Election Cycles

Election results and chamber control are treated as contextual inputs, not causal claims.

## What is tracked

- Change in majority party in the House or Senate
- Change in key committee chairs (Foreign Affairs, Banking, Armed Services, Intelligence)
- Timing relative to the end of a Congress (`died-sine-die` risk)

## Current note (November 2026)

The 119th Congress ends on 3 January 2027.  
Bills that have not been enacted by that date (including the Remote Access Security Act) are marked with sine-die risk. Any reintroduction in the 120th Congress will be recorded as a new signal.

**Midterm result.** Secondary reporting (see the [Sanctions Drop #18](../analysis/sanctions-drop-18-democrats-take-congress.md) and [Al Jazeera](../analysis/aljazeera-democrats-win-congress.md) reading notes) records Democratic control of both chambers from the 120th Congress — provisionally Senate 54 D / 44 R with races uncalled, and a House majority called by the Associated Press (4 November 2026). This is logged as a contextual input pending confirmation against certified results and the House/Senate organising resolutions in January 2027. The Tier 1 figure is the **Chamber composition** table on the [Congressional Signals](../trackers/congress-signals.md) page, refreshed daily from the Congress.gov member endpoint; it will reflect the 120th Congress automatically once members are sworn in. The expected effect on this site's subject matter is in oversight capacity (hearings, letters, confirmations, NDAA), not in stand-alone sanctions legislation, which still faces the Senate filibuster and the veto-override threshold. This reading is consistent across outlets of differing editorial position and across specialist and administration sources — see the [cross-source check](../analysis/midterms-2026-cross-source-check.md); the one contested point is tariffs, where views range from "durable executive authority" to "low-hanging fruit for bipartisan action".

No inference is drawn from the mere fact of an election.
